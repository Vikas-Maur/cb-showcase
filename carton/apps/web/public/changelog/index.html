#7Preview

Graphics stay on the video, and clip names stay readable

Two drawing bugs from Cardboard itself, found while editing on a Windows laptop: code graphics drifting off the footage in full screen, and clip names drawn on top of each other on the timeline.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Code graphics in full screen

In Cardboard, if the preview is zoomed in or out (⌘ or Ctrl with the scroll wheel, or a pinch) when you go full screen, every code graphic is drawn at the wrong size from the top-left corner: too large and cut off at the right and bottom when the preview was zoomed out, too small with its edges inside the picture when it was zoomed in. The video is drawn right, so the graphic no longer lines up with it. Full screen shows the picture unzoomed, but the graphics were still sized for the zoom. At the default zoom, and in exports, it was always right.

Now what’s drawn over the preview takes the size the picture is actually shown at, in full screen and out of it, and stays in place when you zoom inside full screen or leave it.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Clip names drawn once

In Cardboard, when a clip starts just off the timeline’s left edge, its name is drawn twice on top of itself: once at the clip’s start, cut by the edge, and once pinned at the edge, so neither can be read. It happens on any screen, while scrolling or at rest. Now each name shows once: it scrolls off with its clip, then appears at the edge.

Full screen: checked with Cardboard’s unchanged editor code and with ours, zoomed out, at the default zoom and zoomed in, paused and playing. Clip names: checked frame by frame while scrolling, in Cardboard’s own timeline and ours, at 1×, 1.25× and 2× screen scaling. Cardboard’s code for both is the same on a Mac, in 1.4.1 and in 1.5.0.

#6Preview

History, redesigned: compare, find and go back

We rebuilt Carton’s History after testing it in the real app. Every version reads in plain words, any two versions can be compared right in the editor, and you can go back as little as one clip.

History you can read

Each version says who made it, when, and what changed, in an editor’s words, and where it is in the edit: Muted “1.mp4”, at 0:05. Your own run of edits is one row, and an agent at work shows as a live row. An agent’s turn shows your request at once, then a short title of what it did. Play all plays every change in its place on the timeline, with sound, and Before shows how it was.

Compare in the editor

Compare takes over the stage: Side by side, Wipe with a line you drag, or Flip by holding B. Play both plays the two versions in step, shot by shot, so a trimmed or moved clip stays the same shot. The changes are marked on the timeline. It works between cuts too, and you can close History for more room while Compare stays on.

Find any version

Search by clip name, text on screen, what you asked an agent, or who made it. The versions people remember are marked as landmarks with a small picture: Exported, Sent, Named and each agent turn. Exported and Sent are shared with your teammates too. Press and hold a row to peek at it on the stage.

Go back as little as you need

Go back… offers four ways, smallest first, and each says what it will do before anything happens: bring back one clip as it was, undo an agent’s turn, restore the whole edit, or start a new cut from that version.

Sharing needs only your name

Share now fills in the live server for you, so sharing a project takes your name and one click.

Improvements
  • Agents get the same tools. cb history --find finds a version, cb restore --clip brings back one clip, and cb compare --show opens Compare in the editor for you.
Fixes & housekeeping
  • Many smaller fixes from testing on a Windows laptop: clearer words throughout (a mute reads “Muted”, a code change never quotes code), Compare’s layout on smaller screens, Esc and keyboard focus, title-bar tabs that fit narrow windows, and Play all saying when there’s nothing to play.

History’s redesign is in preview, like the rest of Carton.

#5Preview

Sharing: edit one project together, live

Share a project and edit it with your team at the same time. Everyone’s edits show up as they happen, and nobody’s work overwrites anyone else’s.

Share from the title bar

Click the Share icon in Cardboard’s title bar, enter the live server’s address and your name, and the project is shared. Then make invite links that can edit, comment, or only watch. Teammates paste the link under Join a shared project, and the project appears in their library with its history.

See each other work

Edits arrive as they happen, and avatars show who’s in the project. Each person’s edits are shown under their own name, and a teammate’s agent as theirs: “Leo’s Director”.

Keep working offline

Lose the connection and keep editing: your edits wait (“Offline: 3 edits waiting”), then sync when you’re back. If someone changed the same thing meanwhile, you get a conflict card instead of a lost edit.

Improvements
  • Light on the network. Footage stays on your computer until you share it. Small preview copies go up first, in short pieces a teammate can play while the rest uploads; full originals only when someone needs them.
  • Runs on Cloudflare’s free plan. The live server runs on Cloudflare, or on any computer with Node. Storage, the server and sign-in are plug-ins that can be swapped.

Sharing is in preview and needs Carton on, which it is by default (CARDBOARD_CARTON=0 turns it off). Names are typed when you share or join; sign-in, a members list and email invites come later.

#4Preview

Carton: version history for video

Cardboard keeps a short recent history of a project’s timeline. Carton keeps every version of it, with who made each change. You and your agents can now work on the same project without overwriting each other.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Nothing gets overwritten

In Cardboard, two saves at nearly the same moment, say yours and the Director’s, can quietly undo one of them, even when they changed different clips: each save writes the whole project, with no check for a save in between. Now every change is saved against the version it was made on. Changes to different things are combined. When two changes touch the same thing, both are kept and a conflict card asks you to pick, in plain words.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Every version, and who made it

Cardboard keeps only the newest 20 snapshots of a timeline, one for each save that changes it. Carton’s History keeps every version. Each one says who made it: You, a teammate, the Director, Claude Code or Codex. Each Director turn is named after your prompt. Compare any version with the current one, watch the changes play, or restore it.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

An agent’s old copy can’t undo newer edits

An agent edits a copy of the timeline. In Cardboard, once the version that copy came from has dropped out of its history (the newest 20 changed versions), its edit is merged against the current timeline instead, so its old values can quietly replace newer edits, with no conflict shown. Now an edit is always merged against the version it came from.

Undo only what’s yours

Ctrl+Z undoes your own edits and the agent turns you started, never a teammate’s. A whole Director turn can be undone at once.

Cuts: try another version of the edit

Make a separate cut, like a 30-second version or a client version, without copying any media. When you like it, fold all of it, or just part of it, back into the main cut.

Made to drop into Cardboard

Carton is one TypeScript module with no outside dependencies. It runs on Mac, Windows and Linux, in browsers and on Cloudflare. Every outside service it uses is a plug-in that can be swapped.

Improvements
  • New cb commands. cut, fold, credits, restore, compare, conflicts, resolve and publish. Git’s words work too: branch, merge, log, blame, revert.
  • Tools for agents. The Director and your own agents get cuts, folds, credits and publishing as tools.
  • Credits. Select a clip to see who last changed it, down to the subagent.
  • A steady timeline for agents. While the Director works, the timeline file it reads doesn’t change under it. Your edits carry on in the editor and meet its edits through Carton.
  • Bring in their changes. Fold a teammate’s exported .cbproj into your project.
Fixes & housekeeping
  • Exported .cbproj files never carry your history database or this computer’s paths.
  • Opening a project on Windows retries when antivirus or the search indexer briefly holds a file.
  • At final integration, all 24 test jobs passed (every test group on Linux and Windows, plus macOS): 6,844 tests, 0 failures.

Carton is in preview and on by default. CARDBOARD_CARTON=0 turns it off, and with it off the editor works as it always has.

#3Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

An idle editor that stays idle

With a project open and nothing moving, Cardboard still did work on every screen refresh: up to 144 times a second on a 144 Hz screen, in every open project tab of our Windows build. Now the editor rests when nothing moves.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

This is Cardboard’s own editor code, not our port. In Cardboard on a Mac, while a project’s editor is on screen, it measures where the preview sits on every frame, even when nothing moves.

20.3% → 0.7%of a CPU core, one project open, window on screen, nothing moving
64.8% → 2.0%of a CPU core, four projects open, window on screen, nothing moving
Unchangedplayback: about 143 frames per second and 1.1 cores, before and after

It still reacts at once when something could move the preview: resizing the window or a panel, zooming or panning the preview, dragging. It keeps up while things move, and rests half a second after they stop.

Measured on a laptop with a 144 Hz screen, plugged in: the build before against the build after, 5 alternating runs each, medians (4 each for playback). A separate re-run with different tools got similar results. A window that’s completely covered was already paused by Windows, in both builds.

#2

Smoother playback, and a prompt box that behaves

We played real projects on a Windows laptop and fixed what got in the way.

26–37 → 78–88frames per second for a graphic with a video inside, while playing, with both changes below
17–20 → 4–6editor stutters over 50 ms in 19 s of that playback, from using the NVIDIA GPU

Measured on a Windows laptop with two GPUs, plugged in: the build before against the build after, 3 alternating runs each, each in its own fresh instance.

Graphics play smoothly

A graphic with a video inside waited for each exact video frame before drawing the next, even with the video off screen, and froze for seconds when a decode was slow. While playing, it now draws with the newest frame that’s ready: on the same GPU, that alone took it from about 40 to about 73 frames per second. Paused or scrubbing, you still see the exact frame.

The fast GPU on laptops

On a laptop with two GPUs, Cardboard drew everything on the power-saving one, which borrows system memory, while the NVIDIA card sat idle. Plugged in, Cardboard now uses the NVIDIA card. On battery it stays on the power-saving one, and Windows’ own per-app setting still wins.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Play means play

In Cardboard, Play after a seek can wait for sound that hasn’t loaded yet from clips on muted tracks, which will never be heard, and pressing Play again while it waits doesn’t skip the wait. Now it waits only for sound you’ll hear, and a second press starts at once.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Prompt badges stay whole, and open on a click

In Cardboard, a badge in the Director’s prompt box (a clip or file mentioned with @, an attachment, a link, a skill) can be clicked or arrowed into, and typing there breaks it into loose text. Now a badge is one piece that the caret steps over. Click one to open it: a clip is selected on the timeline, a file opens in Media preview, a link opens in your browser.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Bugs Cardboard has too

  • The playhead stays in view. In Cardboard, stepping frames with ← and → walks it off the edge of the timeline, and dragging it past the edge doesn’t scroll. Now the timeline scrolls to follow.
  • Clicking the timeline takes the keyboard. In Cardboard, it stays in the prompt box, so Space types a space instead of playing.
  • The left rail shows its tooltips. In Cardboard, its icons never show their names.
  • The timeline redraws after a GPU reset. In Cardboard, if the timeline’s drawing surfaces are lost and restored, which can happen when the graphics process restarts, the timeline can stay black until something else redraws it.
Fixes & housekeeping
  • A benchmark kit, and a “baseline” build of Cardboard’s own pipeline to compare against, so every speed claim can be re-run on the same machine.
  • A study re-implementation of how Cardboard merges an edited timeline: the groundwork for Carton.

#1

Cardboard on Windows

Cardboard only ships for Apple Silicon Macs. We turned its Mac app into a Windows app, with Windows versions of every tool it bundles. It’s one download, with nothing to install.

Feels like a Windows app

The tab strip is the title bar, with Windows’ own minimize, maximize and close buttons, Snap Layouts included. The app menu opens from Cardboard’s logo, or with Alt or F10, and every menu shortcut still works.

The Director works on Windows

The built-in Claude Code agent, its Python tools and the cb command line work from Git Bash, cmd and PowerShell. Cardboard also finds the Claude Code and Codex you installed yourself.

Your NVIDIA GPU does the heavy lifting

Imported video is decoded and encoded on the GPU, with an automatic fallback to the CPU.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Timeline thumbnails from keyframes

To draw a video’s thumbnails along the timeline, Cardboard decodes every frame of it, then keeps one every half second or second. Now, when a video has a keyframe at least every 2 seconds, only the keyframes are decoded; otherwise every frame is, on the NVIDIA GPU when there is one.

A Windows keyboard

Ctrl+Y redoes, the Shortcuts page draws a Windows keyboard, and a new shortcut that clashes with another one is caught.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Import a whole folder

In Cardboard, a folder dropped on the editor imports no media, and its media import pickers can’t choose a folder. Now use File → Import Folder…, or drop a folder on the editor or the Dashboard. Its videos, audio and images come in, subfolders included, in name order.

Also in Cardboard 1.5.0

Bugs Cardboard has too

  • Enter answers the Director’s questions. In Cardboard, clicking an option and then pressing Enter un-picks it, so nothing is sent. Now Enter on a picked option sends the answer.
  • Interrupted imports finish their thumbnails. In Cardboard, a video whose import is cut off after its first thumbnail, by a crash or a forced quit, never gets the rest, even when the project reopens. Now it gets them when the project reopens.
Fixes & housekeeping
  • Imports no longer lose clips. Windows refuses to replace a file something is reading, so a save during an import could fail and drop a clip. Saves now retry.
  • Closing the window quits Cardboard. After a failed import step it used to keep running with no window.
  • Notifications show on Windows, and the taskbar button flashes when the Director needs you.
  • Cardboard says “this PC” and “File Explorer” where it said “this Mac” and “Finder”.
  • An activity log records how a session went: shortcuts, menus, waits and errors. Never what you type.
  • One script builds the whole app from scratch, and a GitHub workflow publishes it as a single self-extracting download.

Still Mac-only: automatic updates, Gemini Argon, and the one-click install for Claude Code and Codex. Install those two yourself, then press “Check again” in Settings → Agents.

Speed numbers come from recorded runs on one Windows laptop, against the build before the change; the ones in #1–#7 were measured on Cardboard 1.4.0. Every number here was checked independently before it went up. The bugs marked “Also in Cardboard” were found by reading Cardboard’s code; we have no Mac, so none of them was measured on one.
